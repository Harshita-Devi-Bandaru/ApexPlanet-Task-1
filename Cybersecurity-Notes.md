# Cybersecurity Fundamentals

## 1. CIA Triad

### Confidentiality
Confidentiality means protecting information from unauthorized access.

Example:
Only authorized employees should be able to access confidential company files.

### Integrity
Integrity means ensuring that information is accurate and has not been modified without authorization.

Example:
A financial transaction should not be changed by an unauthorized person.

### Availability
Availability means ensuring that systems and information are accessible when authorized users need them.

Example:
A company's website should remain available to legitimate users.

## 2. Threat Types

### Phishing
A social engineering attack where an attacker attempts to trick users into revealing information or performing an unsafe action.

### Malware
Malicious software designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to systems.

### DDoS
A Distributed Denial-of-Service attack attempts to make a service unavailable by overwhelming it with traffic or requests.

### SQL Injection
An attack where malicious SQL input is used to manipulate a database query.

### Brute Force
An attack that attempts many possible passwords or credentials until the correct one is found.

### Ransomware
Malware that can encrypt or otherwise restrict access to data and demand payment from victims.

## 3. Attack Vectors

### Social Engineering
Manipulating people into revealing information or performing an action.

### Wireless Attacks
Attacks targeting wireless networks or communications.

### Insider Threats
Security threats originating from someone who has legitimate access to an organization's systems or information.
